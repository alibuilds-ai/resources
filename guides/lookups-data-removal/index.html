Lookups.io: what it knows about you, and how to get removed

Lookups.io merges hundreds of public-record databases into one profile per person — name, phones, addresses, relatives, emails. If a profile with your data exists, one form on their data-claim page gets it taken down, typically within an hour of confirming your email.

People-search sites scrape public records — county filings, phone carriers, social signals — and stitch them into searchable profiles. Most make removal deliberately tedious. Lookups.io is the rare one with a single, fast claim process, which is exactly why it is worth knowing both sides of it: what it aggregates, and the four-step opt-out.

What Lookups.io does

  • Unified search. One query by name, phone, address or email hits 250+ public and licensed data sources at once, instead of you checking site by site.
  • Smart merging. The engine de-duplicates and links scattered records into a single connected identity per person.
  • Identity intelligence. Full name plus aliases, date of birth and age, and relatives and associates from its person graph.
  • Phone intelligence. Mobile, landline and VOIP numbers with carrier data, verified live against telecom networks rather than pulled from a static dump.
  • Location history. Current and historical addresses, residential and business.
  • Digital footprint. Associated email addresses, social handles and online-presence indicators.
  • Scale. 500M+ records, sub-100ms average response, real-time API access for high-volume users.
why it matters

Everything above cuts both ways. The same report that helps an investigator find someone is a stranger's one-click view of your home address history and family connections. Checking whether you're on it — then claiming your data off it — takes about five minutes.

Remove your data: the claim process

  1. Find yourself first

    Search for your own name (and common aliases) on lookups.io. Note the URL of every profile that is actually you — they look like https://lookups.io/p/123. You need these links for the claim form.

  2. Open the data-claim page

    Go to lookups.io/data-claim.

  3. Paste the profile link

    Add the link to the profile you found your data under — e.g. https://lookups.io/p/123. Repeat for each separate profile if more than one came back as you.

  4. Enter your email address

    This is where the confirmation lands. Use an inbox you actually check.

  5. Confirm the email

    Click the confirmation link they send. Once confirmed, the removal goes through — typically within an hour.

After the claim

  • Re-check in a week. Search your name again to confirm the profile is gone before moving on.
  • Repeat per profile. One claim per profile link — don't assume one submission clears every variant of your name.
  • You'll likely need this again elsewhere. Lookups.io sources from public records; other aggregators rebuild profiles independently. Treat this page as the template: find profile → find opt-out form → paste link → verify email → confirm removal.
if you copy one thing

The confirm-email step is load-bearing. Plenty of removal requests die silently because the verification email was never clicked — the claim only starts counting after you confirm.

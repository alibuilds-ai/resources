Lookups.io: what it knows about you, and how to get removed

Lookups.io merges hundreds of public-record databases into one profile per person: your name, phones, addresses, relatives, emails. If a profile with your data is up there, one form gets it taken down, usually within an hour.

People-search sites scrape public records and stitch them into searchable profiles. Most make removal tedious on purpose. Lookups.io has a single fast claim process instead, so it's worth knowing both sides of it: what it aggregates, then the opt-out.

What Lookups.io does

  • Unified search. One query by name, phone, address or email hits over 250 public and licensed data sources at once, so you don't check them site by site.
  • Record merging. The engine de-duplicates scattered records and links them into one identity per person.
  • Identity intel. Legal name plus aliases, date of birth and age, relatives and associates from its person graph.
  • Phone intel. Mobile, landline and VOIP numbers with carrier data. These get verified live against telecom networks, not pulled from a static dump.
  • Location history. Current and past addresses, residential and business.
  • Digital footprint. Associated email addresses, social handles, online-presence indicators.
  • Scale. 500M+ records, sub-100ms average response, real-time API access if you're doing volume.
why it matters

The same report that helps an investigator find someone gives a stranger a one-click view of your address history and family. Checking whether you're on the site takes a couple of minutes, and claiming your data off it takes about five.

Remove your data: the claim process

  1. Find yourself first

    Search your own name (and any aliases you go by) on lookups.io. Copy the URL of every profile that's actually you. They look like https://lookups.io/p/123. You need these links for the form.

  2. Open the data-claim page

    Go to lookups.io/data-claim.

  3. Paste the profile link

    Add the link you found your data under, e.g. https://lookups.io/p/123. If more than one profile came back as you, do one claim per link.

  4. Enter your email address

    This is where the confirmation lands, so use an inbox you actually check.

  5. Confirm the email

    Click the confirmation link they send you. Once that's done the removal goes through, typically within an hour.

After the claim

  • Re-check in a week. Search your name again and confirm the profile is actually gone.
  • One claim per profile. Don't assume one submission clears every variant of your name.
  • You'll do this again elsewhere. Lookups.io pulls from public records, and other aggregators build their profiles independently. The pattern is the same everywhere though: find your profile, find the opt-out form, paste the link, verify your email, confirm the removal.
if you copy one thing

The confirm-email step is where most removal requests die quietly. The claim only starts counting after you click that verification link.
